Modeling Entanglements: Entanglement Links vs. Entanglement Springs¶

In polymer network elasticity simulations using the Maximum Entropy Homogenization Procedure (MEHP) (Gusev [Gus19], Bernhard and Gusev [BG25]), topological entanglements impose constraints on polymer chain fluctuations.

In MEHPForceBalance2, users can choose between two distinct representations for sampled entanglements via the entanglements_as_springs parameter:

  1. Entanglement Links (entanglements_as_springs=False, default): The two entangled strand beads are merged into a single shared 4-functional junction node (\(\mathbf{r}_1 = \mathbf{r}_2\)), equivalent to an instantaneous tetrafunctional cross-link. This imposes a rigid spatial constraint, effectively pinning the chains together at a localized junction. Importantly, in Force Balance 2, these links cannot slip along the chain contour; they remain fixed at their sampled contour positions.

  2. Entanglement Springs (entanglements_as_springs=True): The two entangled strand beads remain distinct and are coupled by an additional Hookean spring. This introduces internal elastic compliance between the chains, allowing them to fluctuate at finite separation distances. Like entanglement links, entanglement springs cannot slip along the chain contour.

Note

Nomenclature Clarification: In earlier literature and colloquial discussion, these models have sometimes been loosely called “slip-links” or “slip-springs”. However, in MEHPForceBalance2, the links and springs do not slip along the polymer chains. In Bernhard and Gusev [BG25], they are referred to simply as entanglement links (or entanglement beads) and entanglement springs. The first-generation MEHPForceBalance did provide an option for links to slide dynamically along the contour (disable_slipping=False), but slipping was found to artificially soften the network modulus and add unnecessary numerical complexity. For a detailed comparison of actual slipping vs. fixed links, see Entanglement Mobility: Dynamic Slipping vs. Fixed Links.

This example illustrates:

  • Topological Differences: Number of extra nodes, degrees of freedom, and springs introduced.

  • Elastic Compliance & Scaling: How the shear modulus \(G\) scales with entanglement density under both models.

  • Statistical Significance: Multi-seed ensemble sampling with 95% confidence intervals.

  • Physical Guidance: When to choose entanglement links versus entanglement springs.

Background & Mathematical Formulation¶

When an entanglement is identified between two strands \(s_1\) and \(s_2\) at contour positions \(\alpha_1\) and \(\alpha_2\):

  • In the Entanglement Link representation: A single node is added at position \(\mathbf{R}_{\text{link}} = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{r}_1 + \mathbf{r}_2)\). Both strands are partitioned at this node into sub-springs:

    \[N_{\text{extra nodes}} = N_{\text{ent}}, \quad N_{\text{extra springs}} = 0\]

    Because the constraint is rigid, any force transmitted across the entanglement directly stresses the network backbone without internal deflection.

  • In the Entanglement Spring representation: Two separate nodes are placed at \(\mathbf{r}_1\) and \(\mathbf{r}_2\), and an explicit Hookean spring connects them:

    \[N_{\text{extra nodes}} = 2 N_{\text{ent}}, \quad N_{\text{extra springs}} = N_{\text{ent}}\]

    The entanglement spring acts in series with the strand segments, adding compliance and reducing the effective stiffness of the entanglement constraint.

Multi-Seed Simulation Procedure¶

To ensure statistically meaningful conclusions, we generate independent Monte Carlo network realizations across multiple random seeds and sample entanglements stochastically.

To keep CI execution and documentation builds fast (< 1 s) while allowing deep statistical sampling (multiple seeds and complex systems), the results are cached into a committed .npz archive and loaded automatically. You can rerun the live multi-seed computation at any time by passing --recompute or setting PYLIMER_RECOMPUTE_CACHE=1.

def _simulate_link_vs_spring_single_repeat(
    r: int,
    seed: int,
    box_l: float,
    b: float,
    n_strands: int,
    strand_len: int,
    conversion: float,
    density_factors: Sequence[float],
    nominal_n_ent: int,
    cutoff: float,
    r02: float,
    vol: float,
    gamma_conv: float,
) -> Tuple[int, np.ndarray, np.ndarray, np.ndarray, np.ndarray]:
    """Execute a single Monte Carlo network realization across entanglement densities."""
    gen = MCUniverseGenerator(box_l, box_l, box_l)
    gen.set_seed(seed)
    gen.set_bead_distance(b)
    gen.set_mean_squared_bead_distance(b**2)
    gen.add_strands(n_strands, [strand_len] * n_strands)
    gen.add_crosslinkers(int(0.5 * n_strands), 4)
    gen.link_strands_to_conversion(conversion)

    n_factors = len(density_factors)
    g_links = np.zeros(n_factors)
    g_springs = np.zeros(n_factors)
    extra_links = np.zeros(n_factors)
    extra_springs = np.zeros(n_factors)

    for i, factor in enumerate(density_factors):
        n_target = int(factor * nominal_n_ent)

        if n_target == 0:
            # Bare unentangled crosslinker network
            fb_bare = gen.get_force_balance2()
            fb_bare.run_force_relaxation()
            g_pa = (gamma_conv * fb_bare.get_gamma_factor(r02) / vol) * 1e6
            g_links[i] = g_pa
            g_springs[i] = g_pa
            extra_links[i] = 0
            extra_springs[i] = 0
        else:
            # Sample entanglements using seeded Brownian bridge
            es = gen.sample_entanglements(
                n_target, cutoff, 0.0, 0, 3.0, f"ent_{seed}_{factor}", True
            )

            # 1. Entanglement Links (merged junction nodes)
            fb_l = gen.get_force_balance2(
                es, crosslinker_type=2, entanglements_as_springs=False
            )
            extra_links[i] = fb_l.get_nr_of_extra_atoms()
            fb_l.run_force_relaxation()
            g_links[i] = (gamma_conv * fb_l.get_gamma_factor(r02) / vol) * 1e6

            # 2. Entanglement Springs (explicit Hookean coupling springs)
            fb_s = gen.get_force_balance2(
                es, crosslinker_type=2, entanglements_as_springs=True
            )
            extra_springs[i] = fb_s.get_nr_of_extra_atoms()
            fb_s.run_force_relaxation()
            g_springs[i] = (
                gamma_conv * fb_s.get_gamma_factor(r02) / vol) * 1e6

    return r, g_links, g_springs, extra_links, extra_springs


def run_entanglement_link_vs_spring_simulation(
    density_factors: Sequence[float] = (0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0),
    n_strands: int = 10000,
    strand_len: int = 50,
    conversion: float = 0.95,
    n_repeats: int = 10,
    base_seed: int = 123,
    max_workers: Optional[int] = None,
) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    """Evaluate shear modulus and extra degrees of freedom across entanglement densities.

    Averages over independent network realizations and stochastic entanglement samplings
    to provide statistically robust comparisons with Student's t 95% confidence intervals.
    """
    params = get_parameters_for_polymer(
        "PDMS", parameter_type=ParameterType.GAUSSIAN)
    b = params.get("<b>").to(params.get("distance_units")).magnitude
    bead_density = params.get_bead_density()
    cutoff = params.get_sampling_cutoff()
    nominal_ent_density = params.get_entanglement_density()
    r02 = params.get("R02").to(params.get("distance_units") ** 2).magnitude
    kbt = params.get("T") * params.get("kb")
    gamma_conv = (
        (kbt / ((params.get("distance_units")) ** 3)).to("MPa")).magnitude

    n_atoms = n_strands * strand_len + int(0.5 * n_strands)
    vol = n_atoms / bead_density
    box_l = vol ** (1.0 / 3.0)
    nominal_n_ent = int(nominal_ent_density * vol)

    all_g_links = np.zeros((len(density_factors), n_repeats))
    all_g_springs = np.zeros((len(density_factors), n_repeats))
    all_extra_links = np.zeros((len(density_factors), n_repeats))
    all_extra_springs = np.zeros((len(density_factors), n_repeats))

    tasks = [
        (
            r,
            base_seed + 101 * r,
            box_l,
            b,
            n_strands,
            strand_len,
            conversion,
            density_factors,
            nominal_n_ent,
            cutoff,
            r02,
            vol,
            gamma_conv,
        )
        for r in range(n_repeats)
    ]

    workers = min(resolve_max_workers(max_workers), n_repeats)

    if workers > 1 and n_repeats > 1:
        print(
            f"Executing {n_repeats} repeats in parallel across {workers} worker processes..."
        )
        completed = 0
        with ProcessPoolExecutor(max_workers=workers) as executor:
            futures = [
                executor.submit(_simulate_link_vs_spring_single_repeat, *task)
                for task in tasks
            ]
            for fut in as_completed(futures):
                r, g_l, g_s, ex_l, ex_s = fut.result()
                all_g_links[:, r] = g_l
                all_g_springs[:, r] = g_s
                all_extra_links[:, r] = ex_l
                all_extra_springs[:, r] = ex_s
                completed += 1
                print(
                    f"  [Link vs Spring] Completed repeat {completed}/{
                        n_repeats
                    } (repeat index {r + 1})..."
                )
    else:
        for task in tasks:
            r, g_l, g_s, ex_l, ex_s = _simulate_link_vs_spring_single_repeat(
                *task)
            all_g_links[:, r] = g_l
            all_g_springs[:, r] = g_s
            all_extra_links[:, r] = ex_l
            all_extra_springs[:, r] = ex_s
            print(
                f"  [Link vs Spring] Completed repeat {
                    r + 1}/{n_repeats}...")

    # Compute statistical moments and 95% confidence intervals across seeds
    stat_links = compute_ensemble_statistics(all_g_links, axis=1)
    stat_springs = compute_ensemble_statistics(all_g_springs, axis=1)
    stat_extra_l = compute_ensemble_statistics(all_extra_links, axis=1)
    stat_extra_s = compute_ensemble_statistics(all_extra_springs, axis=1)

    return {
        "density_factors": np.array(density_factors),
        "n_repeats": np.array(n_repeats),
        "nominal_n_ent": np.array(nominal_n_ent),
        "vol": np.array(vol),
        "g_links_mean": stat_links["mean"],
        "g_links_std": stat_links["std"],
        "g_links_sem": stat_links["sem"],
        "g_links_ci": stat_links["ci"],
        "g_links_all": all_g_links,
        "g_springs_mean": stat_springs["mean"],
        "g_springs_std": stat_springs["std"],
        "g_springs_sem": stat_springs["sem"],
        "g_springs_ci": stat_springs["ci"],
        "g_springs_all": all_g_springs,
        "extra_atoms_links_mean": stat_extra_l["mean"],
        "extra_atoms_links_std": stat_extra_l["std"],
        "extra_atoms_springs_mean": stat_extra_s["mean"],
        "extra_atoms_springs_std": stat_extra_s["std"],
    }

Visualizing Mechanical & Structural Differences¶

The plots below compare:

  1. Equilibrium Shear Modulus :math:`G` vs. Entanglement Density: Entanglement links exhibit a strong, near-linear increase in modulus with entanglement density due to rigid node coalescing. In contrast, entanglement springs display a softer, sublinear response that saturates because the intermediate spring provides series elastic compliance. Shaded error bands and error bars represent the 95% Student’s t confidence interval computed across independent network and sampling realizations.

  2. System Degrees of Freedom (Extra Nodes): Entanglement springs introduce exactly twice as many extra node coordinates into the linear system of equations as entanglement links.

if __name__ == "__main__" or "sphinx_gallery" in sys.modules:
    fig, (ax_mod, ax_dof) = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(11, 4.5))

    # Modulus comparison with shaded 95% confidence intervals and error bars
    ax_mod.plot(
        density_factors,
        g_links_mean,
        "s-",
        color="#d95f02",
        linewidth=2,
        markersize=6,
        label=f"Entanglement Links (Merged Nodes, N={n_repeats})",
    )
    ax_mod.fill_between(
        density_factors,
        g_links_mean - g_links_ci,
        g_links_mean + g_links_ci,
        color="#d95f02",
        alpha=0.2,
        label="95% Confidence Interval",
    )
    ax_mod.errorbar(
        density_factors,
        g_links_mean,
        yerr=g_links_ci,
        fmt="none",
        ecolor="#d95f02",
        capsize=4,
        elinewidth=1.2,
    )

    ax_mod.plot(
        density_factors,
        g_springs_mean,
        "o-",
        color="#1b9e77",
        linewidth=2,
        markersize=6,
        label=f"Entanglement Springs (Hookean Springs, N={n_repeats})",

    ax_mod.fill_between(
        density_factors,

        color="#1b9e77",
        alpha=0.2,
    )
    ax_mod.errorbar(
        density_factors,
        g_springs_mean,
        yerr=g_springs_ci,
        fmt="none",
        ecolor="#1b9e77",
        capsize=4,
        elinewidth=1.2,

    ax_mod.set_xlabel(

        color="#1b9e77",
        linewidth=2,
        markersize=6,

Practical Recommendations¶

Feature

Entanglement Links (entanglements_as_springs=False)

Entanglement Springs (entanglements_as_springs=True)

Constraint Nature

Rigid point constraint (\(\mathbf{r}_1 = \mathbf{r}_2\)).

Compliant harmonic coupling (\(k_{\text{spring}} > 0\)).

Modulus Response

Higher; scales more steeply with entanglement density.

Softer; exhibits saturation due to series compliance.

System Size (SLE)

Smaller (\(+ N_{\text{ent}}\) nodes, \(0\) extra bonds).

Larger (\(+ 2 N_{\text{ent}}\) nodes, \(+ N_{\text{ent}}\) extra springs).

Recommended Use

Standard affine/phantom homogenization where entanglements act as effective crosslinks (Bernhard and Gusev [BG25]).

Studies exploring inter-chain interaction compliance or finite-range topological coupling.
