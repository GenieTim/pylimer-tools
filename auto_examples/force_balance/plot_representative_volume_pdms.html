Representative Volume Study for PDMS Networks¶

When predicting the equilibrium shear modulus \(G\) of polymer networks via Monte Carlo network generation (MCUniverseGenerator), on-demand entanglement sampling (sample_entanglements()), and MEHPForceBalance2, the simulation box must be large enough to act as a Representative Volume Element (RVE)—avoiding both finite-size bias (e.g. periodic self-looping or insufficient topological sampling) and excessive sample-to-sample variance.

Here we study realistic Gaussian bead-spring models of polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS, \(\rho_{\text{bead}} \approx 2.23\,\text{nm}^{-3}\), \(\rho_{\text{ent}} \approx 0.0608\,\text{nm}^{-3}\), \(\langle b^2 \rangle \approx 1.11\,\text{nm}^2\)) across cubic box sizes \(L \in [14, 110]\,\text{nm}\) (from \(\sim 6\times 10^3\) up to \(\sim 3\times 10^6\) beads), varying:

  1. Chain length \(N\),

  2. Cross-link conversion \(p\),

  3. Stoichiometric imbalance \(r = f N_{\text{xlinks}} / (2 N_{\text{strands}})\), and

  4. Number of independent samples \(n_{\text{samples}}\) needed for a target precision.

Single PDMS Realization with On-Demand Entanglement Sampling¶

Using get_parameters_for_polymer() with ParameterType.GAUSSIAN, we parameterize a realistic PDMS box of side length \(L = 25\,\text{nm}\), sample entanglements on demand, and evaluate \(G\) with MEHPForceBalance2.

params = get_parameters_for_polymer("PDMS", parameter_type=ParameterType.GAUSSIAN)
assert isinstance(params, Parameters)
dist_unit = params.get("distance_units")
b = params.get("<b>").to(dist_unit).magnitude
b2 = params.get("<b^2>").to(dist_unit**2).magnitude
r02 = params.get("R02").to(dist_unit**2).magnitude
gamma_conv = params.get_gamma_conversion_factor().to("MPa").magnitude

box_l = 25.0
vol = box_l**3
strand_len, p_demo, r_demo = 100, 0.95, 1.0
n_strands = int(round(params.get_bead_density() * vol / (strand_len + 0.5 * r_demo)))
n_xlinks = int(round(0.5 * r_demo * n_strands))

gen = pt.MCUniverseGenerator(box_l, box_l, box_l)
gen.set_seed(42)
gen.config_nr_of_mc_steps(0)
gen.set_bead_distance(b)
gen.set_mean_squared_bead_distance(b2)
gen.add_strands(n_strands, [strand_len] * n_strands)
gen.add_crosslinkers(n_xlinks, 4)
gen.link_strands_to_conversion(p_demo)

es = gen.sample_entanglements(
    nr_of_entanglements_to_sample=int(params.get_entanglement_density() * vol),
    upper_cutoff=params.get_sampling_cutoff(),
    seed="demo_rve",
    filtering_mode=pt.EntanglementFilteringMode.OMIT_INACTIVE,
)
fb = gen.get_force_balance2(es, entanglements_as_springs=False)
fb.run_force_relaxation(pt.StructureSimplificationMode.INACTIVE_THEN_X2F)
g_demo = gamma_conv * fb.get_gamma_factor(r02, nr_of_chains=1) / vol
print(
    f"Demo PDMS box (L={box_l:.0f} nm, N_strands={n_strands}): "
    f"G = {g_demo:.4f} MPa, w_active = {fb.get_active_weight_fraction():.3f}"
)

cache_path = get_example_cache_path(
    "representative_volume_pdms_cache.npz", "force_balance"
)
data = load_or_compute_cache(cache_path, run_representative_volume_pdms_study)

Effect of Chain Length (\(N\))¶

Varying the precursor strand length \(N \in \{50, 100, 200, 400\}\) at fixed \(p = 0.95\) and \(r = 1.0\) shows that longer chains require significantly larger physical box sizes \(L\) to converge. Because the unperturbed end-to-end distance scales as \(R_0 = \sqrt{N \langle b^2 \rangle}\) (reaching \(\sim 21\,\text{nm}\) at \(N = 400\)) and the cross-link density drops as \(1/N\), small boxes (\(L \lesssim R_0\)) overestimate \(G\) and exhibit large sample-to-sample variance. Normalizing by \(L / R_0\) shows that a box size of \(L \gtrsim 3\text{--}4\,R_0\) reduces the coefficient of variation (CV) below 2–5%.

plot_rve_chain_length_study(data)

Effect of Cross-link Conversion (\(p\))¶

Varying the cross-link conversion \(p \in \{0.75, 0.85, 0.925, 0.98\}\) at \(N = 100\) and \(r = 1.0\) approaches the classical gelation threshold \(p_{\text{gel}} = 1 / \sqrt{r(f - 1)} \approx 0.577\) from above. Near \(p = 0.75\), the active weight fraction drops sharply and spatial connectivity fluctuations grow, inflating both the finite-size bias in small boxes and the sample-to-sample CV by roughly an order of magnitude compared to well-cross-linked networks (\(p \ge 0.925\)).

plot_rve_conversion_study(data)

Effect of Stoichiometric Imbalance (\(r\))¶

Varying the stoichiometric ratio \(r = f N_{\text{xlinks}} / (2 N_{\text{strands}})\) across \(r \in \{0.65, 0.80, 1.00, 1.25\}\) (each reacted to 95% of its stoichiometric maximum conversion, \(p = 0.95 \min(1, 1/r)\)) demonstrates a similar broadening of the required RVE under cross-linker deficiency (\(r = 0.65\)), where sparse percolation paths increase sample-to-sample scatter. By contrast, excess cross-linker (\(r = 1.25\), corresponding to 95% strand conversion) preserves a high active fraction and converges nearly as fast as the stoichiometric reference (\(r = 1.00\)).

plot_rve_stoichiometry_study(data)

Number of Samples vs. Representative Volume¶

For the reference PDMS network (\(N = 100\), \(p = 0.925\), \(r = 1.0\)), we evaluate \(n_{\text{samples}} = 24\) independent realizations at each box size. While the 95% confidence interval half-width decreases as \(1/\sqrt{n_{\text{samples}}}\), averaging many small boxes (\(L \le 20\,\text{nm}\)) cannot eliminate systematic finite-size bias. Choosing \(L \ge 56\,\text{nm}\) (\(\sim 4\times 10^5\) beads) with \(n_{\text{samples}} \approx 8\text{--}12\) independent realizations keeps both finite-size bias and statistical uncertainty below 1–2%.

plot_rve_sample_count_study(data)
